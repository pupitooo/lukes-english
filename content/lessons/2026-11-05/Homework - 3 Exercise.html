The chart

Five boxes. Every verb lives in at least one of them. When you are not sure, find the verb here first.

1. verb + TO

We decided to wait. I can't afford to lose this client.

afford, agree, aim, appear, arrange, ask, attempt, be able, beg, can't wait, choose, claim, consent, dare, decide, demand, deserve, expect, fail, guarantee, happen, hesitate, hope, hurry, learn, long, manage, need, offer, opt, plan, prepare, pretend, proceed, promise, prove, refuse, seem, strive, struggle, swear, tend, threaten, turn out, volunteer, vow, wait, want, wish, would like, would love, would hate, would prefer

verb + somebody + TO

I want you to check it. The client asked us to send it again.

advise, allow, ask, beg, cause, challenge, convince, enable, encourage, expect, force, get, help, hire, instruct, invite, need, order, pay, permit, persuade, remind, require, teach, tell, tempt, train, trust, urge, want, warn, would like

Czech chci, abys = I want you to. Never "I want that you". Also too / enough + to: too small to break anything, not enough time to test it.

2. verb + -ING

I suggest testing it first. Would you mind sharing your screen?

admit, anticipate, appreciate, avoid, can't face, can't help, can't stand, carry on, celebrate, consider, contemplate, delay, deny, detest, discuss, dislike, end up, enjoy, escape, excuse, face, fancy, feel like, finish, forgive, give up, imagine, involve, justify, keep, keep on, mention, mind, miss, postpone, practise, put off, quit, recall, recommend, report, resent, resist, risk, suggest, support, tolerate, understand

phrases + -ING

it's worth, it's no use, there's no point (in), spend time, waste time, have trouble, have difficulty, be busy, look forward to, be used to, get used to, object to, when it comes to, be committed to

In the bold phrases to is a preposition, and after a preposition always -ing (class 2): used to working, look forward to meeting. Czech navrhuju, abychom = I suggest checking or I suggest that we check. Never "suggest to" and never "suggest us to". Nedokážu si představit, že bych = I can't imagine working.

3. TO or -ING, same meaning

I started to learn Scala. = I started learning Scala.

begin, start, continue, like, love, hate, prefer, can't bear, bother, intend, cease

But would like / love / hate / prefer is always TO: I'd prefer to work from home tomorrow. And with knowing and understanding verbs use TO: I'm starting to understand.

4. TO or -ING, different meaning

verb+ TO+ -ING
remembera task, still to do: Remember to back up the database.a memory: I remember deploying it.
forgetDon't forget to tell the client.I'll never forget seeing that error.
stopstop in order to: I stopped to buy a coffee.quit: Stop refreshing the page.
tryan effort, often failed: I tried to open it, but it was locked.an experiment, to see what happens: Try clearing the cache.
regretformal bad news: We regret to inform you...sorry about the past: I regret saying it.
meanintend: I meant to call you.involve: Scala means learning new things.
go onthe next thing: Then he went on to present the results.continue the same: He went on talking for an hour.
needactive: I need to rewrite it.passive meaning: The code needs refactoring.
be afraidtoo scared to do it: I was afraid to ask.afraid it may happen: I'm afraid of losing data.
advise, allow, permit, recommendwith a person: They allowed us to work from home.no person: They don't allow working from home.

5. NO TO

Let me check. He made us rewrite it. You'd better call him.

can, could, may, might, must, shall, should, will, would, needn't, had better, would rather, why not, rather than, do / does / did, let somebody, make somebody, have somebody (get it done), help somebody (TO is optional), see, hear, watch, notice, feel somebody

Czech nech mě = let me explain, never "let me to explain". Přinutil nás = he made us rewrite, but in the passive TO comes back: we were made to rewrite it.
