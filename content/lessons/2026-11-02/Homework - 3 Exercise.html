💻 Quantifiers: much, many and friends

The grammar

1. Can you count it?

Countable nouns have a plural: a bug, two bugs; a user, users; a request, a ticket, a client, an invoice, a meeting, a question.

Uncountable nouns have no plural and take a singular verb: time, data, memory, code, work, money, traffic, feedback, information, advice, software, knowledge, vocabulary, progress.

Czech trap: informace, rady, data, slovíčka feel plural in Czech. In English they are not: the information is, a piece of advice, my vocabulary (never vocabularies), the software is. And práce is two words: work (uncountable, the activity) and a job (countable, the position).

3. The four rules that fix most mistakes

  1. much and many live in questions and negatives. In a normal positive sentence say a lot of: "We have a lot of data", not "we have much data". (The only positive exceptions: so much, too much, too many, so many.)
  2. a few / a little is positive: there are some. few / little without "a" is negative: almost none. "A few people came" (good) vs "Few people came" (disappointing).
  3. fewer for things you count, less for things you don't: fewer meetings, less time.
  4. much + comparative: much faster, much more expensive, much better. Never "many more expensive". But many more + plural noun: many more users.

Czech hodně = a lot of (positive), much / many (question, negative). Czech málo = little / few, or naturally not much / not many. Czech trochu, pár = a little, a few. Czech tolik = so much / so many. Czech mnohem + comparative = much.
