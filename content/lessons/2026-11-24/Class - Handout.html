The day the film nearly disappeared

In 1998, a computer animation studio in California was working on the sequel to its first big hit. The film was due in cinemas the following year, and the files for every character, set and shot sat on one shared file system.

One day, an animator was looking at one of the main characters on his screen when he noticed something strange. The character's hat had disappeared. A few seconds later, his boots were gone too. Somebody had run a delete command on the main project folder, and it was quietly removing files, one after another.

By the time someone pulled the plug on the server, most of the film's files were gone. Two years of work had vanished in a few minutes. The team went straight to the backups, and that was when they found out the second problem: the backup system had been failing for weeks, and nobody had noticed.

The film was saved by a lucky accident. One of the technical directors had had a baby a few months earlier and had been working from home. To do that, she had been copying the whole film to a computer at her house. Her copy was only days old.

A small team drove to her house, wrapped the computer in blankets, put it carefully on the back seat and drove it back to the studio, slowly. Over the next few days they rebuilt the film from that one machine and filled in the gaps by hand.

In the end, much of the film was reworked anyway, because the studio decided the story wasn't good enough. It came out in 1999 and became a huge hit. Since then, the story has become a classic among engineers, and people still tell it whenever somebody says "we have backups".
